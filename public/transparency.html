Plain-language status: the public directory is operating. Donations, personal need intake, user accounts, provider dashboards, volunteer registration, and case management are not operating through this website.

1. What RWN currently provides

RWN helps a visitor discover public information about essential services, review practical access details, open the original source, and launch a map provider. Search, filters, saved-service identifiers, optional distance sorting, sharing, printing, and downloads run in the visitor’s browser.

RWN is not emergency dispatch, a service provider, an eligibility authority, a healthcare provider, or a guarantee that a listed service has space when someone arrives.

The reviewed directory and bounded live feeds are counted separately. At the August 5, 2026 audit, the official USDA 2026 feed contained 17,442 sites active that day, including 637 in Massachusetts. A user’s browser loads at most the nearest 300 current records after a U.S. location search so the globe stays responsive.

Coverage is not evenly distributed. Some markers represent a physical service entrance; others represent an official locator or a network with wider reach. Record counts are not an impact claim, and RWN does not claim how many people obtained help because of the directory.

3. Open directory data

The complete reviewed directory is available without operating the 3D globe. These exports contain public service facts, coordinates, review dates, dataset identifiers, and source URLs; they do not contain visitor information. Seasonal live-feed records remain at the official source and are loaded in bounded, date-filtered searches.

4. How listings are controlled

Every published record must include a stable identifier, service or locator name, public place or coverage description, coordinates, listing type, aid categories, access information, source name, HTTPS source URL, and ISO-formatted review date.

Release checks reject duplicate identifiers, invalid coordinates, unsafe source protocols, missing operational fields, implausible dates, and broken production builds. Human review remains necessary because automated checks cannot decide whether a provider page actually supports the wording of a listing.

Read the full Editorial Policy for the source hierarchy, inclusion standard, coordinate rules, freshness approach, and correction requirements.

5. Privacy and accessibility

The site has no account, advertising network, marketing cookie, analytics SDK, personal case form, or donation processor. Optional exact device location is used in memory for distance sorting. For current U.S. meal sites, the browser sends USDA’s ArcGIS service a point rounded to one decimal degree only after location approval. See the Privacy Notice.

The 3D globe is supplementary. The text directory exposes every reviewed listing without WebGL or application JavaScript. RWN is engineering toward WCAG 2.2 Level AA but does not claim formal conformance. See the Accessibility Statement.

6. Governance and organizational status

A professional humanitarian organization should publish its legal entity, jurisdiction, registration or tax status, leadership and board accountability, public contact routes, safeguarding owner, complaints process, financial reports, funding disclosures, conflicts policy, and measurable impact methodology.

Those organization-level facts have not been verified for publication on this site. RWN therefore does not present invented officers, registration numbers, donation totals, partner endorsements, beneficiary counts, annual reports, or a legal address.

Required before expansion: authorized leadership and counsel must verify the entity and public contacts, assign safeguarding and correction responsibilities, approve the public policies, and establish financial controls before the site accepts money or personal requests.

7. Technology and map provenance

RWN is a static website hosted on Vercel. The application does not require an RWN database to search the directory. Production releases run automated syntax, data, test, and build checks and use restrictive browser security headers.

The globe uses locally hosted NASA Blue Marble imagery and Natural Earth country geometry. A lightweight boundary layer loads first; more detailed 1:10m geometry loads only when someone zooms closer. Natural Earth boundaries are generalized and can reflect cartographic viewpoints; they are not legal or navigational authority.

Map and imagery sources: Natural Earth downloads and NASA Blue Marble: Next Generation.
