Editorial policy

Useful information, tied to a source.

A listing should tell a person what help exists, where the information came from, how to take the next step, and how recently it was checked.

Standard adopted August 5, 2026Applies to every public listing
On this pagePurposeSourcesInclusionLocationsReviewCorrectionsIndependence

The core rule: if RWN cannot name a credible public source, support a no-cost access path, and explain how a person takes the next step, the listing is not ready to publish.

1. Purpose and audience

RWN organizes public information about free essential services so a person can find a practical next step. A record must be a free service, a free service for eligible participants, a genuine zero-dollar option, or free official navigation. The directory prioritizes clarity under stress: plain names, need categories, access details, hours or availability notes, public contact routes, original sources, review dates, and map options.

RWN is a discovery service. It is not a provider accreditation, a clinical judgment, an eligibility decision, or an endorsement of every practice of a listed organization.

2. Source hierarchy

Editors should use the strongest available evidence in this order:

  1. an official government, public-agency, or provider service page;
  2. an official provider location finder, program page, or public directory;
  3. a recognized humanitarian network’s official listing;
  4. a first-party public document or announcement that can be dated; then
  5. a reputable secondary directory only as a lead that must be confirmed against a stronger source.

Every public RWN record must include a source name, HTTPS source URL, and last-reviewed date. Search-engine snippets, AI-generated summaries, anonymous posts, and copied directory text are not enough on their own.

3. What may be included

A listing may be included when it offers or reliably connects people to free essential help such as food, water, shelter, healthcare, hygiene, clothing, crisis support, or closely related navigation. The public source must describe both the help and the no-cost access path. Eligibility, referrals, vouchers, age limits, and voluntary-contribution models must be labeled rather than described as universally free.

A listing should be excluded or removed when no-cost access cannot be supported, the only relevant service is fee-based or merely discounted, the source is unverifiable, the service appears closed, the organization misrepresents what it offers, a link is unsafe, the marker is only an administrative office without a public access route, the primary purpose is commercial lead generation, or the listing could create a material safety risk. Paid placement does not determine inclusion or ranking.

4. Location and marker standards

Coordinates are checked against the written place and source context. Live USDA records must pass the official map’s geocoding threshold and remain paired with their written address. National boundaries use Natural Earth generalized geometry and do not express an RWN political position or provide legal boundary guidance.

5. Review and freshness

For a reviewed snapshot, the date shown is the date an editor or controlled importer last checked the public source—not a promise that the provider confirmed every field that day. A listing labeled as a live official feed shows when the browser retrieved an active record from the named agency source.

Seasonal records must carry active dates when the source supplies them. The finder excludes USDA summer-meal records before their start date or after their end date. Missing hours are labeled as missing; RWN does not infer an opening schedule.

Automated checks reject unsafe URLs, invalid coordinates, duplicate source identifiers, repeated categories, missing no-cost language, unsupported free-access models, missing fields, impossible dates, malformed source responses, and suspicious record-count drops. Human review remains necessary to understand access language and source meaning. When hours or capacity can change quickly, the listing asks the visitor to confirm before travel.

6. Corrections and removals

Corrections should be evidence-led and leave a clear audit trail in the source data. The preferred evidence is an updated first-party page, provider notice, official location record, or direct confirmation from an authorized provider representative.

For an urgent safety problem, a listing can be temporarily removed while evidence is reviewed. Cosmetic or ranking requests do not override accuracy and user safety.

Current reporting boundary: a verified public RWN correction channel has not yet been established. Providers should first correct their official public source. RWN must publish a monitored correction address and response process before representing the editorial operation as fully governed.

7. Independence and conflicts

RWN does not currently accept donations through this website and does not offer sponsored placement. If funding or partnerships are introduced, they must not buy inclusion, favorable language, map prominence, or a higher ranking. Material conflicts should be disclosed and handled by someone who did not create the conflict.

Metrics should measure whether people can find credible next steps—not reward sensational language, unnecessary data collection, or engagement for its own sake.
